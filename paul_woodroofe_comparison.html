This compares your April baseline with your August retest. The headline is a good one: your jump height, the single number that stood out as your main area to grow last time, has moved up, and your relative strength has held firm on a lighter frame. That is a strong place to be for anyone, and particularly so in your 60s. Two things are worth knowing before the detail. Those two are best confirmed with a standardised jump next time; they are shown below for reference rather than scored.

What the Data Shows
The picture since April is a positive one. Your jump height, which was the clear outlier last time, has risen from 12.9 cm to 15.5 cm, and that has lifted you off the bottom of the over-50 group. Your lower body strength has held its ground: the raw pull is a touch lower, but you are carrying nearly five kilograms less, so relative to your bodyweight you are a fraction stronger than in April. Your leg symmetry stays excellent, and your balance has improved. In your 60s, and with your hip history, seeing strength maintained and balance moving the right way is exactly what you want to see.
🔄
Confirm the Jump Metrics With a Standardised Retest
Before we read anything into reactive strength or power, we want a jump done with a consistent countermovement depth and tempo. The August readings for those two look like they came from a deeper or slower jump on the day, which distorts both numbers. A standardised jump at your next visit will give us a clean read and let us track them properly from there. Everything else in this report stands on its own.
